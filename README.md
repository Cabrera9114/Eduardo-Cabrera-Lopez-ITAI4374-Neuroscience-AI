## What I Learned

In this module, I learned that modern artificial intelligence systems are heavily inspired by neuroscience but still operate very differently from the human brain.

One of the most important concepts I learned was the difference between dense and sparse computation. Modern AI systems often activate entire networks during processing, while biological brains use sparse computation where only small groups of neurons activate when needed. This makes the brain significantly more energy efficient.

I also learned that human learning is continuous and adaptive, unlike many AI systems that rely on separate training and deployment phases. The brain constantly updates itself based on experience and environmental interaction.

Another important topic was the concept of NeuroAI, which combines neuroscience and artificial intelligence research to develop more efficient, adaptable, and biologically inspired AI systems.

The research articles helped me better understand how neuronal diversity, memory systems, and biological intelligence can influence future AI architectures.

## Challenges

One challenge during this module was understanding how neuroscience concepts translate into artificial intelligence models. Since biological neural systems are much more complex than artificial neural networks, it required careful reading and comparison between the two fields.

Another challenge was learning the terminology related to computational neuroscience and biological neural systems.
