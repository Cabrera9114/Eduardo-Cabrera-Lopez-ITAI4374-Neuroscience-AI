## Free Energy Principle and Active Inference

The module introduced Karl Friston’s Free Energy Principle, which explains behavior as an attempt to minimize surprise.

The brain can reduce surprise in two ways:

1. Update its internal model to match reality.
2. Act on the world to make reality match its prediction.

This concept helped me understand active inference, where agents act not only to receive rewards but also to reduce uncertainty and prediction error. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Brain World Models

The module explained that several brain regions contribute to world modeling:

- Hippocampus: simulates future scenarios and supports mental time travel
- Cerebellum: predicts motor outcomes using rapid forward models
- Prefrontal cortex: evaluates plans and goals
- Visual cortex: generates predictions about sensory input
- Basal ganglia: selects actions based on predicted value

This showed how imagination, planning, and prediction are deeply connected in the brain. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## Model-Based vs. Model-Free Reinforcement Learning

This module helped me understand the difference between model-free and model-based reinforcement learning.

Model-free reinforcement learning learns mainly through trial and error. Model-based reinforcement learning builds an internal model of the environment and uses that model to plan before acting.

This connects directly to the difference between habitual behavior and goal-directed behavior in the brain. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
