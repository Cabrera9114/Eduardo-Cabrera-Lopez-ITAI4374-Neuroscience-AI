## Course Information

- Course: ITAI 4374 – Neuroscience as a Model for AI
- Instructor: Professor Patricia McManus
- Semester: Spring 2026

---

## Project Overview

This project was a semester-long neuroscience-inspired audit of a real-world AI system.

The objective was to evaluate how closely modern AI systems reflect principles found in biological intelligence and neuroscience.

The assignment required students to:
- select a real AI system
- design neuroscience-inspired experiments
- compare AI behavior to human cognition
- analyze strengths and weaknesses
- propose neuroscience-inspired improvements

The project was divided into:
1. Midterm Pilot Audit
2. Final Comprehensive Audit + Video Demonstration

# Key Findings

## Strengths of ChatGPT

- Strong language generation
- Fast contextual reasoning
- Good pattern recognition
- Strong symbolic reasoning
- Effective multi-domain knowledge recall
- Good instruction following
- Strong text summarization

## Weaknesses Compared to the Human Brain

- No true embodiment
- No physical sensory experience
- Limited long-term memory persistence
- No biological emotions
- Weak causal reasoning in unfamiliar environments
- Vulnerable to hallucinations
- Limited self-awareness
- No true proprioception or sensorimotor learning

# Reflection

This project completely changed how I think about artificial intelligence.

Before this course, I mainly viewed AI systems as powerful software tools. Through this audit, I learned how deeply neuroscience influences modern AI architectures and how much current AI systems still differ from biological intelligence.

One of the biggest lessons I learned was that modern AI systems can imitate some cognitive behaviors extremely well while still lacking many essential properties of human cognition such as embodiment, physical experience, emotional grounding, and true adaptive learning.

I also found it especially interesting how concepts like predictive processing, memory systems, embodied cognition, and neuro-symbolic reasoning appear both in neuroscience and modern AI research.

This project helped me better understand the future direction of AI development and strengthened my interest in neuroscience-inspired artificial intelligence and robotics.
